---
title: "Opinion: What is Bon Appétit Feeding Us? It Certainly Isn’t Squirrel;
  But Perhaps It Should Be?"
author: E Peyton
pubDate: Oct 7, 2026
---

With Halloween just around the corner, the usual set of fears are circulating campus: vampires, werewolves, mummies, and ghouls are just a few of the horrors guaranteed to be weighing on students’ minds in October. But arriving with the ordinary frights is a new beast this year; of course, I am referring to the destabilization of America’s food supply.

Recent food deregulations, and the mass product recalls that continue to follow, have made the nation increasingly wary of what's on their shelves. Not a day goes by without something being pulled for possible listeria, salmonella, or E.coli contamination, raising the question of not what will make you sick, but rather, what won’t?

Cornell is no exception. For most students, the dining hall functions as the middleman between food production and consumption. But with production disrupted, distrust toward the beloved Bon has begun to brew. I, myself, have started picking at my meals, wondering when a contaminated ingredient will find its way onto my plate.

Of course, the Bon’s affiliation with Bon Appétit puts it in a better position than most. It has no doubt been their sustainability and local support initiatives that have kept students’ stomachs safe thus far; but these goals have become harder to fulfill as food has grown more untrustworthy. Regardless of the Bon’s current effectiveness, their kitchen could use some relief, and students some reassurance.

There is a popular saying, “kill two birds with one stone.” In the case of Cornell– I suggest we kill, not birds– but squirrels. The idea may seem ludicrous, but what would be far more so is losing the bright minds of this vibrant institution to bacteria-ridden snackage. The squirrels on campus check all the boxes to be considered a sustainable food source; they are simply neverending!

Most enticing of all is the ethicality of the whole operation. A package of squirrel meat could adorn itself with all the labeling associated with a moral meal choice: local, free-range, humanely handled, and organic. With a list like that, you’d be a monster not to chow down! It's everything food should be, and to serve it in the Bon would not only put students at ease, but also aid Bon Appétit in its mission to bring better, healthier options to the table.

Vampires, werewolves, mummies, and ghouls may be just a fact of life, but for Cornell, a destabilized food supply doesn’t have to be. I urge you to support my campaign for tasty squirrel options in the dining hall. Our voices are best heard together.
