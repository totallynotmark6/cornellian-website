With Halloween around the corner, it’s once again time to revisit one of America’s greatest cultural traditions: trash. And as a broke college student, I’m immediately drawn to the modern-day Library of Alexandria that is the free streaming service Tubi. Holding diverse, esteemed titles—ranging from *Se7en* to *Rectuma*—and packing about twenty minutes of ads into eighty-minute movies, Tubi is arguably the greatest creation and undoubtedly the best archive in cinematic history.

As a massive fan of Tubi, I have taken it upon myself to watch one Tubi film per block and review it—mostly to save anyone else from watching them. In the spirit of Halloween and the vaguely polyamorous undertones of Cornell, I have chosen the 2007 film *The Gay Bed and Breakfast of Terror* (*B&B*), directed by Jaymes Thompson. *B&B* follows five same-sex couples who each decide to stop at a seemingly innocuous inn, the Sahara Salvation, on their way to the biggest, queerest party of the year. The eerily Middle-American mom and daughter running the inn serve the guests delicious mincemeat muffins—later revealed to be created from human flesh. The rest of the movie follows the same kind of winkingly nonsensical plot as the mother and daughter hunt down and murder their homosexual guests in a fit of religious fervor. It’s really dumb, but pretty entertaining. Lesbian folk singers, cheating twinks, and assorted other power couples (who all blend together as the movie drags on), all face the wrath of God as carried out by two crazed women.

All in all, *B&B*’s low production value, longer-than-needed runtime, and plethora of stereotypical characters dragged it down, but the absurd plot and lowbrow humor made for a fun watch anyway. This Halloween, get a good group of open-minded friends and enjoy one of the films that made me slightly ashamed to be gay.

**Best Scene:** The orgy at the Republican National Convention, which came out of absolutely nowhere.

**Best Character:** Luella, the kooky and probably mentally addled daughter of the innkeeper, whose facial expressions and actions were entertaining even at the movie’s dullest points.
