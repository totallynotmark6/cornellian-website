title: "The United States' Blockade Affects You Too"
author: Rhye Nelson
pubDate: Wed Apr 29, 2026
---
As a result of the United States and Israel’s attack on Iran on February 28, 2026, oil flowing through the Strait of Hormuz has trickled to almost a complete standstill. The passage is about 21 miles across at the narrowest point (Reuters), connecting the Persian/Arabian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, which eventually reaches the Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean. With a blockade from Iran and an additional blockade from the United States, oil prices have begun to skyrocket. Around 20% of the world’s oil and liquified natural gas passes through the strait from the Gulf states of Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates (BBC). In 2025, approximately 20 million barrels of oil and oil products travel through the Strait of Hormuz per day (US Energy Information Administration)—equivalent to about $600 billion worth of energy trade every year. Of the crude oil and fossil fuels leaving the Strait, 82% goes to Asian countries (US Energy Information Administration).

According to the BBC, one-third of the world’s fertilizer trade passes through the strait. Of the fertilizer imports in the United States, 15% is from the Gulf States (American Farm Bureau). As many farmers in Iowa are getting ready to start planting this year’s crops, fertilizers and nitrogen costs have gone up as much as 40% since last fall (Des Moines Register). As it takes over a month for oil tankers to reach the United States, there is a delayed impact from the closure in early March. Globally, the Asian markets are feeling the loss of oil first, with many countries reopening their coal production while others are turning to nuclear or solar energy. In the United Kingdom, the last known shipment of jet fuel reached their shores on April 7. According to UK consultants and analysts, airlines will be affected near the end of April if there are still restrictions on shipments (Financial Times). While 88% of oil in the United States comes from North America, the global impacts of the United States’ blockade on the strait is disrupting global trade. Within the coming months, the cost for food, gas, flights, energy, and other aspects of daily life will continue to climb as the conflict continues.
